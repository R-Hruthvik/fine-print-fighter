Complaint channels, posting types, and tracking

Research on public consumer complaint channels across India, the EU, and the US for the app’s three dispute packs. Focus: what kinds of complaint posting exist, where complaints can be posted, how status is tracked, and how much the app can automate versus assist.

3. What the app can actually do

The app currently performs the analysis and drafting work, but it does not post complaints or monitor external complaint status. It can monitor its own analysis progress and saved-case records only.

4. How many types the app can do and monitor

  • Complaint packs supported: 3 (flight delay, subscription cancellation, surprise bill/fee).
  • Jurisdictions: 3 (IN, EU, US).
  • Escalation entries: 4 per pack per jurisdiction (unreviewed pack strings).
  • Direct automated complaint postings: 0. All channels require user identity, login, OTP/CAPTCHA, document upload, or jurisdiction-specific steps; no reliable public submission APIs were found.
  • External status monitoring: 0. The app monitors its own analysis progress and saved-case records only; it does not poll company/regulator/court systems.
  • Assisted channels: All listed channels can be assisted through drafting, checklists, evidence packaging, deadline reminders, and status polling where the user supplies credentials/case numbers.

6. Recommended positioning

Position the app as an evidence-first dispute assistant, not a complaint filing service. It can prepare a strong, cited, editable complaint and help the user choose the right channel, but the user must send/file and track externally. Future features could add channel-specific filing checklists, status polling with user-supplied case numbers, and reminders, but full automated submission should remain out of scope unless official APIs and consent flows exist.
